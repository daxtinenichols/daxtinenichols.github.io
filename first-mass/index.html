A guide for newcomers

Your first Mass

Walking into a Catholic church for the first time can feel like arriving at a family dinner where everyone already knows the routine. Here is the routine, so you can relax and simply be there.

Before you go

Four small things to know

Pick a time

Sunday Masses start Saturday evening and run through Sunday. Look up nearby times on MassTimes.org or the parish website.

Arriving ten minutes early gives you time to settle in.

Wear something tidy

Dress the way you would for a nice dinner. You'll see everything from suits to jeans, and nobody is checking.

Walking in

Catholics usually touch the holy water by the door and make the sign of the cross, then bend the right knee toward the altar before stepping into a pew. Join in or simply find a seat.

Where to sit

Somewhere in the middle lets you follow the people in front of you. The book in the pew, often called a missalette, has most of the words.

Step by step

What happens at Mass

Every Mass follows the same shape in four parts. The words people say aloud are under each step, and the companion beside them shows whether to stand, sit or kneel as you read.

      The moment most newcomers wonder about

      When everyone goes up to Communion

      Catholics believe that what the priest offers at Communion is truly Jesus. Receiving him is also a sign of full unity with the Catholic Church, so the Church asks people who aren't Catholic not to receive yet.

      It isn't a judgment of you. It's closer to a family promise you haven't made yet, and the invitation to make it is always open.

      The U.S. bishops explain this in their guidelines for receiving Communion.

      Stay in your seat

      Let people step past you and use the time to pray or simply rest in the quiet. Plenty of Catholics do this too, so nobody will notice.

      Go up for a blessing

      In many parishes you can walk up in line with your arms crossed over your chest. The priest will say a short blessing over you instead.

      Common worries

      Questions people are shy to ask

      What if I do something wrong?

      Everyone in that room had a first Mass once, and many still lose their place. Follow the people around you. If you miss a cue, nothing happens. God is not grading posture.

      Do I have to put money in the basket?

      No. Visitors often just pass the basket along. Parishioners support the parish over time; a guest is not expected to.

      Can I bring my kids?

      Yes. Children are part of almost every Mass, noise included. Many churches have a cry room or a back area if you need to step out for a moment.

      Why is everyone so quiet beforehand?

      Catholics believe Jesus is present in the tabernacle, the gold box usually behind or beside the altar. People pray before Mass, so the quiet is a courtesy, like lowering your voice in someone's home.

      Can I talk to the priest afterward?

      Usually, yes. Many priests greet people at the door after Mass. Saying "This was my first time" is a perfectly good way to start.

      Is a weekday Mass different?

      It's the same shape, only shorter, often about thirty minutes, with fewer songs and one reading before the Gospel. It can be a quieter way to try it.
